CT0  —  The Center0 Feature

CT0

Feature to use Center0 instead of Center1 to maintain Transom direction vertically regardless of Mullion angle.

Mullion Reference

Mullion has a separate direction point, so the Center1 points in Mullion Section are aligned to the start point, end point, and direction point of the Mullion Line.

Section Reference PointMullion Line Alignment Basis
P1Aligns to the start point M1 of the Mullion Line.
P2Vertical direction point of Center1 start point and aligns to the end point M2 of the Mullion Line.
P3Center1 end point and aligns to the direction point M3 of the Mullion Line.

Transom Reference

Transom does not have a separate direction point; it creates direction points based on two Mullions on either side. When both Mullions have the same direction, the midpoint between the two Mullion end points becomes the direction point for Transom Center1.

When the two Mullions have different directions, a midpoint that leans toward neither side is used. In this case, the glass plane of the Mullion and the glass plane of the Transom may not align exactly, and the Transom may be modeled at a slight backward angle.

CT0 Application

Like Unit Head and Sill, when you need to maintain a horizontal or vertical reference regardless of Mullion angle, write the direction line as Center0 instead of Center1. Using the command CT0 ensures that the Transom direction always appears vertical.

Note: Use Center1 for regular Transoms that must follow Mullion direction, and use Center0 for conditions like Head/Sill that must maintain a constant direction.
